# TIMEFOLD — The Folded Worlds

## One coherent world

**The 4D concept is the space and its timeline. The 2.5D concept is the objects living inside each moment.**

A corridor repeats across four eras. The same archive categories persist, but their bodies, materials, contents, and owner change. Time travel should feel like encountering an earlier or later version of something familiar.

The person gives the technological changes a human scale. A smaller child with a backpack becomes a young adult in casual clothes, an adult in a jacket, and an older person with silver hair and a future coat. Each is the same fictional inhabitant of the room.

## Atmosphere

Liminal, quiet, and slightly impossible. Repeated thresholds, soft fluorescent strips, distant doorways, tiled floors, suspended photographs, and paper-thin archive islands create the feeling of being between destinations. Editorial serif typography keeps the archive personal.

Interstellar informs the spatial metaphor for time; liminal/backrooms spaces inform architectural repetition. The tone is contemplative rather than horror. The future is a possible chapter, with deliberately speculative technology and memories.

## Four versions of a life

| Era  | Person              | Clothing direction              | Color direction                              |
| ---- | ------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1998 | Child, age 10       | Backpack and childhood clothing | Brown, cream, and faded red; a warm memory   |
| 2012 | Young adult, age 24 | Casual everyday clothes         | Amber and yellow; experiments and beginnings |
| 2026 | Adult, age 38       | Jacket                          | Neutral white and grey; the present anchor   |
| 2056 | Older adult, age 68 | Silver hair and a future coat   | Blue and black; an imagined future           |

These dates and ages describe an invented person born in 1988. They are sample narrative choices, not claims about the site owner's life.

Lighting, fog, and the interface palette blend during travel. Each chapter has its own architecture colors, object forms, and memories, allowing a visitor to compare clear stages of change. The camera and atmosphere interpolate continuously; the person and object silhouettes are chapter snapshots rather than a continuous biological morph.

## Seven doors

| Persistent category | What changes with the era                      | World inside it                                      |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Code and projects   | Computer technology and the work on its screen | The project at that point in its life                |
| Photography         | Camera form and image collection               | Photographs and captions belonging to that era       |
| Play                | Console technology and the game description    | A playable fragment-collection experiment            |
| Journal             | Writing medium and the entry                   | A thought addressed to another version of the person |
| Food                | Vessel and an everyday ritual                  | A still life and a food memory                       |
| Places              | Map or globe technology and destinations       | Places remembered or imagined                        |
| Person              | Age, proportions, hair, posture, and clothing  | The inhabitant's changing life and perspective       |

These categories are stable identities. The laptop category, for example, does not require a contemporary laptop in every room. Its older version can be a bulky computer and its future version a speculative display. A memory opens from the object associated with its own chapter.

## Color as time

The palette moves **brown/cream/red → amber/yellow → white/grey → blue/black**. Red makes the past feel warm and distant; blue makes the future feel clear, unfamiliar, and open. A neutral present anchors the transition. Green appears as a small supporting accent, leaving each era with a distinct atmosphere.

The direction borrows the language of redshift and blueshift. Observed light shifts toward longer, redder wavelengths when a source recedes and toward shorter, bluer wavelengths when it approaches. This is an artistic analogy, not a physical rule that the past must be red and the future blue. [NASA's Doppler-shift illustration](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/webb/doppler-shift-of-a-stars-spectrum/) explains the underlying observation.

## Interaction rhythm

**Notice → travel → approach → unfold → return.**

A cursor shift reveals depth. Time travel changes the atmosphere and reveals the next version of the room. Hovering identifies an object; clicking moves the camera closer and opens its memories. Closing returns to the current chapter.

The archive index offers direct entry into every category, including the person. It also keeps the archive usable with a keyboard and when WebGL is unavailable. Timeline controls inside the detail view let the visitor compare a category across chapters.

## Authoring the archive

Keep the story, physical object, and atmosphere aligned: an early computer should contain early experiments; an older person should have a later perspective; a future gallery should be framed as speculative when its memories have not happened.

Use [docs/CONTENT.md](docs/CONTENT.md) to add projects, posts, releases, and linked work to each object's collection. The same project can recur across eras with a stable ID, different status, and progress snapshots. This makes creative work part of the timeline rather than a single finished portfolio card.

The scene-editing instructions are in [docs/TIMELINE.md](docs/TIMELINE.md). Begin with the era data, then refine procedural forms or replace selected geometry with local models. Bespoke shaders, frame-to-world unfolding, and detailed Blender assets can build on the same structure.
